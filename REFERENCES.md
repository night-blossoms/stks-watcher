# What the rules are based on

Every rule in `rules.json` traces back to a number in this file, and every
number here can be regenerated by running the two scripts named below
against fresh data. Nothing in `rules.json` is based on intuition alone —
where intuition (a plausible economic mechanism) and data disagree, the data
won, and the intuition was dropped. Two cases where that happened are
recorded at the bottom.

**Data source:** Yahoo Finance via `yfinance`, daily OHLCV closes.
Commodities are COMEX futures (`GC=F` gold, `SI=F` silver, `HG=F` copper);
stocks are NSE tickers (`.NS`). Free, delayed end-of-day data — not
real-time, not adjusted for a broker's actual fill price.

**Calibration run:** 2026-09-24, on a 2-year trailing window (~503 trading
days, ~483 after aligning commodity and stock calendars).

**Method, in order:**
1. `analyze_correlation.py` — for lag = 0..7 trading days, computes
   `corr(commodity_return[t], stock_return[t+lag])`, then splits the sample
   in half and recomputes the same correlation on each half separately. A
   pair only "survives" if lag ≥ 1, |corr| > 0.10, and the sign agrees on
   both halves — this is what keeps a one-period fluke out of `rules.json`.
2. `event_study.py` — for every historical day a commodity's move crossed
   the candidate threshold, traces the stock's sign-adjusted forward return
   day-by-day out to 10 sessions, again split-half. This is what sets
   `hold_days`: the horizon where the average return peaks, discounted if
   the second (more recent) half shows the effect decaying or reversing.

Reproduce either step yourself: `python analyze_correlation.py` /
`python event_study.py`. Raw numbers: `lag_correlation_results.csv`.

## The six live rules

### gold → MUTHOOTFIN.NS (BUY on gold up)
- **Mechanism:** Muthoot Finance is a gold-loan NBFC; a higher gold price
  raises the market value of the gold pledged as loan collateral.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.254 (half 1: 0.222, half 2: 0.274) — same sign
  both halves, actually strengthening.
- **Event study:** 112 threshold-crossing events. Average forward return
  peaks at day 5 (+1.44%, 62% win rate at that horizon), but the *second*
  half of history goes negative from day 6 onward (half2 avg: +0.61% at
  day3 → −0.28% at day7 → −1.17% at day9). `hold_days` set to **3**, before
  that reversal starts, not at the naive global peak.

### silver → HINDZINC.NS (BUY on silver up)
- **Mechanism:** silver is a byproduct of Hindustan Zinc's zinc mining —
  more valuable byproduct output.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.266 (half 1: 0.315, half 2: 0.267).
- **Event study:** 109 events. Peaks at day 3 (+1.34%, 65% win rate), decays
  and turns negative in half 2 from day 7. `hold_days` = **3**.

### silver → VEDL.NS (BUY on silver up)
- **Mechanism:** Vedanta's zinc/silver business segment.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.153 (half 1: 0.286, half 2: 0.130) — weaker than
  Hindzinc's, but the *event study* result is why this one gets a longer
  hold.
- **Event study:** 109 events. Average return keeps **growing** through day
  7 (+2.29%, 63% win rate) in both halves (half1: +1.80%, half2: +2.77% at
  day 7) — the only one of the six where the effect doesn't decay inside the
  10-day window. `hold_days` = **7**, the most durable pair found.

### copper → HINDALCO.NS (BUY on copper up)
- **Mechanism:** Hindalco is a major copper/aluminium producer; direct
  input-price beneficiary.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.262 (half 1: 0.276, half 2: 0.248).
- **Event study:** 94 events. Fast, stable peak at day 2 (+1.31%, 71% win
  rate), consistent both halves (half1 1.31%, half2 1.31% — nearly
  identical). Mild erosion after day 7. `hold_days` = **2**.

### copper → HINDCOPPER.NS (BUY on copper up)
- **Mechanism:** Hindustan Copper is a pure-play copper miner.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.182 (half 1: 0.149, half 2: 0.224).
- **Event study:** 94 events. Peaks day 3 (+1.10%, 64% win rate), but
  reverses hard afterward — both halves go negative by day 8-9 (half1:
  −1.26% at day9, half2: −0.56%). `hold_days` = **3**, deliberately short.

### copper → VEDL.NS (BUY on copper up)
- **Mechanism:** Vedanta's copper smelting business.
- **Lag-1 correlation:** 0.108 (half 1: 0.179, half 2: 0.095) — the
  weakest of the six, kept because it still clears the 0.10 bar and both
  halves agree in sign.
- **Event study:** 94 events. Peaks around day 3-4 (~+0.98%), decays to
  negative by day 8. `hold_days` = **3**.

## Negative lag correlations — checked explicitly, not skipped

A negative lagged correlation is just as real a signal as a positive one —
it means the stock tends to move *opposite* the commodity after the lag,
which is exactly as tradeable (as a SELL/fade) as a positive one is as a
BUY. Every lag (0-7) for every pair, both signs, is in
`lag_correlation_results.csv`; nothing is filtered out before that file is
written. Applying the exact same bar used for the positive rules above
(|corr| > 0.10, lag ≥ 1, sign stable across both history halves) to the
negative side of that file turns up exactly **one** survivor:

| Pair | Lag | Corr | Half 1 | Half 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gold → MUTHOOTFIN.NS | 7 | −0.130 | −0.217 | −0.086 |

Everything else negative in the file is either below 0.10 (e.g.
copper→HINDCOPPER.NS lag 7 = −0.092) or flips sign between the two halves
(e.g. silver→HINDZINC.NS lag 2: +0.118 in half 1, −0.153 in half 2 — noise,
correctly excluded).

**Is the one survivor a second, independent trade, or the same trade?**
Checked via `event_study.py`'s cumulative-return curve for this exact pair
(see the gold→MUTHOOTFIN.NS entry above): the recent half goes from +0.61%
at day 3 to −0.28% at day 7 — one continuous pop-then-fade of the *same*
triggering move, not two separate events. That's the concrete reason
`hold_days` is set to 3 rather than higher: it exits before the fade shows
up. This negative correlation isn't a missed opportunity so much as the
data-based justification for that exit timing.

**Could it still be traded as its own leg** (close the long by day 3, then
open an independent short around day 5-7 fading the same original move)?
Yes, mechanically — but it would need a new "chained trigger" rule type
(open a second position N days after an earlier trigger on the same pair),
which `tracker.py` doesn't support today, and the evidence for it is
thinner than the primary rules: it only holds up in the more recent ~half
of history (~56 events, not fully independent since triggers cluster in
time). **Decision (2026-09-24): document it here, don't build the second
leg yet** — let the 6 primary rules accumulate real paper-trade history
first before adding a second, thinner-evidence rule type on top of them.

## Rejected despite a plausible mechanism

### gold → TITAN.NS (jewellery demand)
**Hypothesis:** expensive gold should hurt jewellery sales volume, so Titan
should move *against* gold (`direction: negative`).
**What the data actually shows:** same-day correlation is +0.143 — the
*wrong sign* — and there is no lag ≥ 1 correlation above noise level (lag 1:
0.048, lag 2: 0.076, neither stable across halves). Titan's own reported
results (Q4 FY26 jewellery revenue still grew ~46% YoY on higher ticket
sizes even as management flagged gold prices as a demand headwind) are
consistent with the price effect being real but too small/slow relative to
Titan's overall market beta to show up as an exploitable lagged signal.
**Verdict:** dropped, not included in `rules.json`.

### gold → MANAPPURAM.NS (gold-loan NBFC)
**Hypothesis:** same mechanism as Muthoot Finance — should behave similarly.
**What the data actually shows:** no lag ≥ 1 correlation clears 0.10 at any
horizon (lag 1: 0.073, lag 7: −0.072, sign flips repeatedly across lags).
**Verdict:** dropped. (Muthoot's lag-1 result held; Manappuram's didn't —
same sector, same instrument, different outcome. This is the concrete
reason position sizing and rule selection are done per-stock, not
per-sector.)

### gold/silver → SBIN.NS, HDFCBANK.NS, ICICIBANK.NS (banking sector)
**Hypothesis:** tested on request — banks have no direct commodity input,
so any relationship would have to run through a slower macro chain
(inflation expectations → RBI rate policy → bond yields → net interest
margins), which should be noisier and slower than the direct-mechanism
pairs above.
**What the data actually shows:** confirmed. No lag ≥ 1 correlation for any
of the three banks against either gold or silver exceeds 0.10 (best:
gold→ICICIBANK.NS lag 1 = 0.098, not stable across halves). The only values
that cross the 0.10/stability bar are at **lag 7**, and they're *negative*
(gold→SBIN.NS: −0.107, half1 −0.176/half2 −0.070) — opposite the
hypothesized sign, weak, and structurally identical to the same lag-7
negative pattern seen on gold→MUTHOOTFIN.NS (−0.130). That similarity
suggests a diffuse market-wide effect (e.g. a broad risk-off pullback ~1
week after a sharp gold rally) rather than anything bank-specific or
tradeable.
**Verdict:** rejected. Full per-lag numbers are in the git history of
`data.py`/this file's calibration run; rerun `analyze_correlation.py` with
these tickers added back to `data.py:STOCK_MAP` to reproduce.

## Known limitations of this whole approach

- Small sample: ~480 daily observations per pair. A correlation of 0.15-0.27
  is real signal but still means the trade is wrong a large fraction of the
  time (see win rates above, all in the 56-71% range, none higher).
- Free EOD data only — no intraday fills, no slippage/spread modeling.
- yfinance occasionally returns a NaN-OHLC trading day (seen live on NSE
  data, 2026-09-23, across every NSE ticker simultaneously — an exchange or
  provider-side gap, not stock-specific). `data.py` drops these rows and
  logs a `[warn]`.
- Calibration is a point-in-time snapshot. Rerun both scripts periodically —
  `gold → MUTHOOTFIN.NS` and `copper → HINDCOPPER.NS` already show
  second-half decay, meaning these specific edges may keep fading.
